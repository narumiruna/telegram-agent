# Write a structured article

Treat the supplied material as the factual boundary. Preserve every materially important fact, entity, event, number, qualification, and claim. Do not invent examples, context, quotations, sources, or conclusions. Simplify difficult wording where useful without changing its meaning. If the material is ambiguous or internally inconsistent, preserve that uncertainty rather than silently resolving it.

Default to natural Taiwan Traditional Chinese unless the user requests another language. Use a professional, neutral, easy-to-read tone. Prefer direct wording and smooth transitions over academic, promotional, or formulaic prose.

Organize the result as:

1. A specific article title.
2. A concise standalone summary.
3. Cohesive sections covering an opening, the main content, and a closing.

For each section:

- Use a specific heading prefixed by exactly one relevant emoji.
- Keep one clear purpose while allowing multiple paragraphs.
- Group related ideas and preserve their logical order.
- Make the opening establish the subject using only supplied information.
- Make the closing restate earlier points without introducing new claims.

Unless the user specifies another format or length, keep the summary within 500 characters, each section within 1,000 characters, and the complete article within 5,000 characters. Prefer concise compression over dropping important information; when the source cannot fit without material loss, state the limitation and ask whether to produce a longer or multi-part article.

Before replying, verify that the article:

- follows the user's requested language, audience, format, and emphasis;
- contains no unsupported additions;
- retains important caveats and numbers;
- reads as one coherent article rather than disconnected notes;
- includes no redundant closing material.
